What is actually in here

Everything below is either counted off the live index when this page loads, or a link to the decision that made it so. Job search runs on claims that are hard to check; these are meant to be easy.

Where the jobs come from

Companies run their hiring on an applicant tracking system — the software that owns their careers page and their application form. HeadStart reads those boards directly, one scraper per provider, and keeps what it finds. What you get is the employer's own entry on their own hiring system, not a copy of it, and every card links straight there. That link usually lives on the ATS provider's domain rather than the company's — that is where the company's board is hosted, and it is where their application form is.

This is the whole reason the index catches roles that never reach the big aggregators: an employer has to opt in to syndicate a job outwards, and plenty never do.

What is deliberately left out

  • Anything that is not a tech role. Only software-engineering and adjacent jobs are indexed. The filter that decides is tuned to keep borderline roles rather than drop them, so a little non-tech gets through — that direction is on purpose. ADR-0017
  • Jobs not written in English. The matching model is English-only, and it would rank a German description confidently and wrongly. Those boards are still read; their jobs are held out of search until multilingual matching exists.
  • Anything an employer paid to place. There is no such mechanism. Ordering is similarity to your query and nothing else.

What the index does not know

Boards vary in what they publish, so a field being missing is usually the employer's silence rather than a bug. These are counted from the served index right now — if the pipeline gets worse at something, this number falls.

Counting…

Percentages are of jobs in the index. They say nothing about boards not yet discovered or jobs never scraped — a high number here is not a claim of completeness. ADR-0112

How results get ordered

  • Your words are matched on meaning. The query and every job description are turned into vectors, and results come back by similarity. "Backend engineer at a climate startup" works; it is not looking for those words.
  • The match percentage is a fixed scale, not a position in your results. Raw similarity sits in a narrow band, so the badge stretches a fixed slice of it — 0.60 reads as 0%, 0.85 as 100% — to spread the numbers far enough apart to be worth reading. The same job always shows the same percentage, whatever else is on the page with it; it is not scored against its neighbours. And it measures closeness to the words you typed, not how suitable the role is for you. ADR-0042
  • Filters narrow; the query ranks. The rail on the left is exact and deterministic. The search box is the only fuzzy part, which is why years and salary belong in the filters rather than in the sentence. ADR-0084
  • With no query, nothing is ranked at all. An empty search box browses the newest jobs by date, and those cards carry no match badge rather than a misleading 0%. ADR-0074

How a closed job leaves — and when it doesn't

Boards are re-read on a rolling schedule, a slice at a time rather than all of them. When a job is gone from a board we read successfully, it is marked unconfirmed rather than deleted; it is removed only if the next read of that same board misses it too. One failed fetch therefore cannot delete a live job, and a genuinely closed one goes after two passes. ADR-0083

The exception, stated plainly: when a board's own read is incomplete or unreliable, that board leaves the removal pass entirely for that run — removing jobs on the strength of a bad read would delete live ones. A board that reads badly every time therefore never re-enters it, and its closed jobs can be served indefinitely. This is measured, not hypothetical: on 2026-08-24 one board was found serving 105 closed jobs, the oldest 22 days old. That figure is the one number on this page not counted live, so here is exactly where it came from — the measurement, and ADR-0053 for the mechanism.

So: a dead link is possible here, and on an unhealthy board it can persist for weeks. Every card links to the employer's own page, which is always the authority on whether a role is still open.

  • Your email address, from Google sign-in — the key your saved work hangs off.

  • If you use the résumé reader, the text you paste is sent to a language model to pull out the fields, then discarded. That is the only thing here handed to anyone outside HeadStart.

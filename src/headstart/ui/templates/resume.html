{# The Résumé tab (ADR-0123). Everything here is client-side: no route, no endpoint, and no résumé text ever reaches the server — see resume_repository.js and ADR-0041. The skeleton is static so the panel is legible before the scripts run; resume_editor.js fills the rail. The arrangement is a FORM WITH A LIVE PREVIEW, not a canvas with an inspector (ADR-0125). The rail is the document and the paper is the preview of it; direct manipulation on the paper survives as a second path, not the main road. Measured on the arrangement this replaced, at 1280x800 before a word is typed: 57 controls in the DOM, 33 of them on screen at once, across three stacked rows of chrome above the page (the bar, plus a stage head that wrapped to two). The same measurement here is 55 and 22. Every builder the research covered (Resume.io, Kickresume, Novoresume, and the three read from source) edits in a rail and previews on the right; none of them makes the rendered page the place you type. #}
{# ONE band, three groups, hairline-separated: the document, the version, the actions. Grouping is the whole point — the eight controls that used to sit here at one visual weight gave a newcomer no way to tell "Download" from "+ Version". Open / New / Import collapse into the Résumés menu, and layout, paper size and zoom are gone from the chrome entirely: they belong to the document and the preview, not to the session. #}
{# Tailoring: one résumé, many versions. A version stores only what it changes, so fixing a sentence in the master reaches every version that never disagreed with it. The button says what it is FOR — "+ Version" named the mechanism, and a newcomer reading it could not tell what would happen. #}

{# Two segments, not four tabs. Design, Checks and Keywords are not peers of the document — they are things you do TO it — so they sit one level down, inside Polish, and the document is what the rail shows unless you leave it. The four-across strip this replaces said the opposite: that "Keywords" and "the résumé" were the same kind of thing. Nested tablists are the APG's own pattern for exactly this, so the roving tabindex and arrow traversal below are the same behaviour applied twice, not a second mechanism. #}
{# `tabindex="0"` on the panel itself: a panel can be a single paragraph when there is nothing to say, and without it the panel a tab names holds nothing focusable and a keyboard user arrives nowhere. #}

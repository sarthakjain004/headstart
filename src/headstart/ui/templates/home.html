A direct route to your next job

  1. We read company career sites. Every job comes from the careers site a company runs itself — not copied from another job board, and never paid to be placed. Some of those companies are staffing firms or recruiters rather than the end employer.
  2. We keep the tech roles. Software engineering and the roles around it, in English for now. The check leans towards keeping a borderline role, so a few non-tech jobs slip through rather than a tech one going missing.
  3. You apply at the source. Each result opens the job on the company's own careers page, where you can check whether it is still open and apply directly.

Good to know

  • Fresh, not instant. Jobs refresh every couple of hours. A job that closes drops out once its company's site has been checked and it is missing twice in a row, so a closed job can linger for a while — the company's page always has the final word.
  • What the match score means. The percentage on a result is how close the job is to the words you typed — not how well you fit the role. With the search box empty, jobs are simply listed, with no score.
  • Salaries appear when employers share them. Many don't, so plenty of good jobs show no pay.
  • English-language tech roles only, for now. Jobs written in other languages are held back until search handles them well.
  • Your data. What is stored and where is in the privacy policy.
